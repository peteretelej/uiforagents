The midnight bulletin

A dark-first briefing identity for data-dense artifacts: near-black warm ground, serif display over a sans body, mono for every label and number, one amber accent spent on what matters now. This page is the identity's own documentation, styled by nothing but its foundation stylesheet.

01 / DESIGN RATIONALE

Decisions, not options

midnight-bulletin exists so an agent building a briefing artifact (a system writeup, a status one-pager, an incident report, a lab note) never improvises the newsprint-of-record look again. The decisions below are extracted from a proven briefing page, not invented.

Night shift, warm bulb. The canonical scheme sits on a near-black warm ground (--bg) with raised surfaces and sunken wells cut from the same hue family - those tokens are verbatim from the extraction source. The light scheme is the derived alternative: warm cream ground, deep bronze accent. Switching themes feels like flipping the office light, not visiting another site.

Serif displays, sans reads, mono counts. Headlines, the dek, and the lede are Charter-led serif. Body copy is system sans at 16px/1.6. Every label, timestamp, table header, pill, and number is system mono - the bulletin counts in mono, the way wire copy does.

One accent, spent on attention. Amber (--accent) marks links, kickers, section eyebrows, timeline dots, and the badge. Green, amber, and red exist only as semantic status in pills, tags, and outcome cells. Nothing decorative gets a color.

The override model. The whole file lives in @layer foundation, so any unlayered page CSS wins without !important. Theme switching needs no attribute dance: tokens use light-dark() and re-resolve against color-scheme on html.

03 / TYPOGRAPHY

Serif says it, mono counts it

The lede opens a section: serif, one size up from body, full ink. Everything after it is sans until a number shows up.

Body paragraphs hold a 68ch measure (--measure) in muted sans; strong text steps up to full ink and doubles as the scan anchor. Section h2 is serif at up to 38px; h3 sub-heads stay sans 16px/600.

Where the mono goes

  • Kickers and eyebrows: 12px mono, letterspaced uppercase, amber.
  • Stat values and outcome cells: 20px and 22px mono, full ink.
  • Table headers, pills, tags, timestamps, captions: 10.5 to 12px mono.

05 / DEBATE

Pro and con, side by side

Compared positions go in .mb-cols panels: green and red mono tags mark the sides, the attribution line sits faint at the bottom. Two panels per debate; a third means the page is hedging.

PRO / DARK-FIRST

Readers of briefings are skimming at night, on second screens, between terminals. A warm near-black ground with full-contrast ink meets them where they already are, and the amber accent reads like a desk lamp.

held by: the night-shift editors

CON / DARK-FIRST

Print is still light, and the light scheme is the one that has to be derived rather than recorded. The bulletin answers with a forced-light print block and a bronze accent tuned to pass contrast on cream.

held by: the print traditionalists

06 / CHRONOLOGY

One rail, amber dots

Sequences render as .mb-timeline: a hairline rail, mono timestamps in accent, muted entries. Timestamps are facts, so they are mono.

09 / BUILD NOTES

How this page was built

  • One stylesheet. The entire <style> block in the head is foundation.css pasted in verbatim. No other CSS exists on this page, so the demo renders identically pasted into a single file or served next to a linked copy.
  • The theme toggle is the only JavaScript. It flips color-scheme on html, and every light-dark() token re-resolves. Without JS the page renders in the identity's lead scheme, dark - the root pin sets it, not the OS preference, and dark is the canonical look; the toggle is how visitors reach the light scheme.
  • No attributes, no per-theme blocks. There is no data-theme attribute anywhere and no duplicated dark token list. Dark is canonical: its tokens are verbatim from the extraction source, and light is derived. (Slot order inside light-dark() is fixed by CSS - the first slot is the light-scheme value - so dark-first means provenance and documentation order, not slot order.)
  • Responsive by collapse. Under 640px the topbar nav hides, the stat strip folds to 2x2, and the outcome grid stacks; under 800px the debate columns stack. Bands keep their hairlines.
  • Printable and calm. Print forces the light scheme through color-scheme, drops the nav and share bar, and keeps boxes from splitting across pages. prefers-reduced-motion is the only motion rule in the file.
