Transit

Harbor line reopens after two-year rebuild

The first train in twenty-six months crossed the drawbridge at 5:41 a.m. Riders say the commute is ten minutes shorter; the city says the tab came in under the bond.

By Mara Ellison and D. Okonkwo · Calder City — Oct. 13, 2026 · 6 min read

The 5:41 was four minutes early and nobody complained, which is the surest sign a thing has been missed. By 6:10 the platform at Union had the look of a normal Tuesday: the same coats, the same thermoses, the same half-dozen people reading over other people’s shoulders. The harbor line, dark for twenty-six months while the drawbridge span and eight stations were rebuilt, was simply a train again.

The ten minutes is the headline the city will keep. The schedule now reads nineteen minutes, Union to Point Annex, against twenty-nine before the closure, and the Harbor Authority puts the saving at 1.4 million rider-hours a year. The bond, $412 million, closed $11 million under its envelope — money the council voted to hold for the ferry settlement rather than return.

The rebuild was never about trains. It was about the ten minutes people get back, every day, without noticing.

— R. Vance, Harbor Authority chief

Not everyone is counting minutes. The Cannery and Foundry stations kept their murals; the Slip 4 platform got the elevator it had been promised since 2011, and the drawbridge operator’s booth is now big enough for two people and a kettle. The dissent at Monday’s council session was about the operating subsidy, not the concrete: two members argued the line’s books should stand alone by 2031.

Colophon · what makes it city-edition

The dense morning daily, not a feed

  • Flat newsprint #F8F7F3, cooler and flatter than a warm cream paper; ink #17150F; boxed modules sit on #FFFFFF and are ruled, never elevated.
  • Every story card carries the full label stack: kicker, serif headline, deck, byline/dateline row. A card missing a layer is not a card of this paper.
  • Hairlines (1px --line) do the everyday work; heavy rules (3px --rule-strong) mark the masthead, section breaks, the lead, and box-outs. No shadows, no gradients, 0px radius.
  • Press red #C0271D is rationed: breaking/live flags, active nav and filter, links, and the one subscribe action — at most one red element per story card, never decoration.
  • Density is the posture: 24px column gaps, rails that read as column zones, agate mono for datelines and most-read. The lead zone is the one place that breathes.
  • Type: Newsreader sets display and body; Archivo Narrow sets uppercase kickers and labels at 0.08em tracking; IBM Plex Mono sets datelines, captions, and agate indexes.
  • Motion is near-static: 160ms fades, underline slides, one slow skeleton pulse. Nothing load-bearing ever moves.
Colophon · decisions

Calls this edition makes

  • Light is the only theme. The dark edition is a different paper, not a token flip, so there are no light-dark() pairs and no toggle.
  • Text-first evidence carries the page: no hosted photography. The one figure is an inline SVG plate, which exercises the figure grammar (hairline frame, fixed ratio, mono caption) without leaving the file.
  • Three surfaces in one document: the front page, a Metro section front with its category filter, and the lead story set in full.
  • The empty well is the reader’s clipping file; the loading row is the wire. States speak newspaper, not spinner.
  • The band of four Metro briefs runs as open columns with hairline dividers — the front page’s column zones — while the boxed card is reserved for the secondary grid and section rails.

Colophon · do / don’t

Keep it in print

Do
  • Set the full label stack on every card, every time.
  • Use heavy rules only where the page breaks: masthead, bands, lead, box-outs.
  • Keep red on a short leash: a flag, an active label, a link, one per card.
  • Set datelines, captions, and indexes in agate mono; decks in muted serif.
  • Let the lead breathe; keep every other zone dense.
Don’t
  • No shadows, gradients, rounded corners, or card elevation.
  • No second accent, no colored fills on cards, no red headlines.
  • No dashboards, charts-of-state, or metric walls — this is a paper, not an instrument panel.
  • No single-story feeds: the unit is the labeled card on a column grid.
  • Never let a skeleton or an empty well invent news; the wire holds space and says so.
